References.

Mathematical background

  1. NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions. Riemann zeta function: §25.2, Definition and Expansions, and §25.4, Reflection Formulas.

    Definitions, analytic continuation, the Laurent expansion at the pole, and the functional equation.

    In this guide: §1.1, zeta, §2.4, the analytic argument, and §14.2.7, the zeta deduction.

  2. NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions. §25.15, Dirichlet L-functions.

    Euler products, primitive and imprimitive characters, finite Euler factors, and the primitive functional equation.

    In this guide: §1.2, characters, §2.5, restoring Euler factors, and §14.2.4, reflection.

  3. NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions. §5.2, Gamma Function: Definitions.

    The gamma function’s continuation, poles and absence of zeros, used when completing an L-function and then removing its gamma factors.

    In this guide: §1.3, completion, and §14.2.5, removing the completion.

  4. J. S. Milne. Class Field Theory, version 4.03, 2020.

    Background on group cohomology, local and global class field theory, and reciprocity. Chapter II develops the cohomological language used to describe global classes and their restrictions.

    Read alongside §3.1, localization kernels, and §5.2, cocycles and coboundaries.

  5. Barry Mazur. “Deforming Galois Representations”, in Galois Groups over ℚ, MSRI Publications 16, Springer, 1989, pp. 385–437. Author-hosted text.

    A foundational reference for organizing lifts of residual Galois representations through deformation theory. The specialized graded cotangent and trace-length comparison in this guide is a further construction in Alpöge’s released source.

    Read alongside §5.3, first-order deformations, and §5.4, the universal ring.

  6. The Stacks Project Authors. “The Koszul complex,” Tag 0621.

    Background on the complex associated to a list of equations and the effect of adjoining one more equation. The specialized graded length comparison is part of Alpöge's released argument.

    Read alongside §8.1.4, quotient-minus-kernel counting, and §8.1.9, changing the order of the cuts.

  7. Jack A. Thorne. On the vanishing of adjoint Bloch–Kato Selmer groups of irreducible automorphic Galois representations, 2022, arXiv:2207.04925.

    Theorem 6.1 supplies the vanishing statement under cyclotomic-kernel irreducibility. The proof uses a deformation–Hecke comparison; Proposition 6.12 concludes with freeness and semisimplicity. The guide distinguishes this mathematical mechanism from the released source's specific rank-three application.

    Read alongside §7.5, why the rank-three polarized adjoint Selmer group vanishes, especially auxiliary primes and dual Selmer control and the patched module, freeness, and faithful action.

  8. James Newton and Jack A. Thorne. Adjoint Selmer groups of automorphic Galois representations of unitary type, 2019; revised 2023, arXiv:1912.11265.

    Background for the dual local conditions and finite-coefficient comparison in §2.4. The later Thorne paper supplies the auxiliary block conditions under cyclotomic-kernel irreducibility used in this guide.

    Read alongside §7.6.1, the dual Selmer problem, §7.6.10, cotangent generators, and §7.7.3, flatness of the patched module.

  9. Sylvain Brochard. Proof of de Smit's conjecture: a freeness criterion, Compositio Mathematica 153 (2017), 2310–2317.

    Theorem 1.1 transfers flatness across a local ring map when the target's embedding dimension does not exceed the source's, under its nonzero-module and finiteness hypotheses.

    Read alongside §7.7.7, which checks these hypotheses for the first-order patched module before descending to the original deformation problem.

This is a selective bibliography for the current exposition. Exact source links for theta matching, arithmetic geometry and the other deeper inputs remain with their respective technical sections and in the source index below.
